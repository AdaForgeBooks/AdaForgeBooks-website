Demise: A Legacy Undone

The Mercer Family

A father's legacy passes into the hands of three grown children. What follows depends less on the will than on the people asked to honor it.

Family Patriarch

Samuel Mercer

Samuel spent forty years building and restoring a small collection of rental homes in Rogersville. A carpenter, landlord, and hands-on problem solver, he treated tenants like people rather than account numbers and taught Daniel to do the same.

His death begins the novel, but Samuel remains present in nearly every decision that follows. The properties are his life's work, and the will is his final attempt to leave his children something stable enough to outlast him.

The Eldest Son

Daniel Mercer

Daniel stays closest to home and closest to Samuel's way of doing things. An Army veteran who spent years helping his father maintain the rentals, he knows the houses, the tenants, and the quiet routines that kept everything working.

He is practical, watchful, and reluctant to interfere until the evidence becomes too close to ignore. What happens only a few houses down the road eventually forces him to choose between family loyalty and protecting the legacy his father trusted them to preserve.

The Middle Child

Nathan Mercer

Nathan lives in Cincinnati, roughly six hours from Rogersville. He was once expected to handle the estate, but distance changed the family's plans before Samuel died.

He trusts Emily completely and assumes the estate is being handled the way their father intended. For a long time, distance makes it easy to accept reassuring answers. Eventually, the same distance becomes the reason he realizes how much he has not been seeing.

Executor

Emily Mercer

The youngest Mercer is handed the role neither brother wanted: executor and keeper of the estate. At first, she approaches the responsibility with care and intends to honor exactly what Samuel left behind.

But grief, bills, pressure at home, and the temptation to solve one problem with money meant for another begin changing the way she thinks. Emily's downfall does not start with one grand betrayal. It begins with decisions that seem temporary, understandable, and easy to fix later.

Emily's Husband

Tyler

Tyler is drawn into the estate through Emily and becomes increasingly connected to the money, repairs, and projects surrounding the Mercer properties.

He is comfortable spending, comfortable promising, and far less interested in finishing work than he is in being paid for it. As the estate begins funding more of their private life, Tyler becomes one of the forces that makes it harder for Emily to step back from what she has started.

A Property in Freefall

The Whitfields

One rental becomes the clearest physical evidence of how far things have slipped. See the house before the tenancy, the damage that followed, and the unfinished attempt to put it back together.
