About the Book

Her Sky Had Seasons: Mamma's Story

A fictional drama drawn from the life of a real woman.

Sherry Fitzgerald Thorne was born on September 15, 1946, in Monroe, Louisiana, the daughter of a war-scarred father who came home from Belgium carrying something he would never name, and a mother who built warmth around his silence with the same steady hands she used to run her beauty shop. From the front steps of that Monroe house, a girl with a Coke bottle full of sweet tea looked out at the world and decided she was going to have more of it.

She spent the next seventy-four years going to get it.

Her Sky Had Seasons is the story of Sherry Thorne told through the eyes of her youngest son, a fictional drama drawn from the life of a real woman who loved fiercely, moved constantly, cooked for everyone within reach, and lived every season of her life with a completeness that most people never manage in a lifetime.

She married Gerald Thorne at seventeen and spent twenty-five years building a life across Louisiana, Virginia, Alabama, and the Tennessee mountains, raising two sons, planting gardens, filling kitchens with good food, and making friends wherever the work took them. She hid in her brother's trunk to get to a drive-in. She navigated across town blindfolded and came back first. She said I was driving to a police officer without hesitation. She dug puppies out of the mud in the rain at midnight. She turned around in a football stadium and dismantled a man who criticized the marching band.

She also lived with bipolar disorder at a time when the illness had no name she knew, when the medication was imprecise and the understanding was incomplete, when the darkness came without warning and the light sometimes burned too bright. She danced barefoot in a rainstorm drinking rainwater from magnolia petals. She sat in a Howard Johnson's parking lot on a night she did not survive the way she planned to. She drove from Florida to Tennessee with twenty-eight thousand dollars in a McDonald's cup. She loved a man who was wrong for her for longer than she should have, because he saw her in the specific way she had always needed to be seen.

And then, after twenty-one years apart, she came home to Gerald. They sat on the back deck of the farmhouse he had rebuilt with his hands, watching the deer come out of the tree line in the east Tennessee afternoon light, and it was enough. It was more than enough.

This is not a perfect life story. It is a true one, in all the ways fiction can be true, which means it holds the hard parts alongside the warm ones and does not resolve the contradictions because the contradictions were real. Sherry Thorne was difficult and generous, maddening and magnificent, the kind of woman who filled every room she entered and left a specific absence when she was gone.

She died on February 16, 2021.

She held on until her son had a chance to say goodbye.

Then she let go.

Her Sky Had Seasons is a love letter from a son to his mother, and an honest account of what it means to live fully inside a life that asks more of you than you sometimes have to give.

She gave it anyway.

Every season.
All the way through.
