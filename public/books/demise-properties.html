Demise: A Legacy Undone

The Mercer Properties

Samuel Mercer did not simply leave houses behind. He left decades of work, responsibility, and memories tied to a quiet stretch of Rogersville, Tennessee.

← Back to Demise

These properties form the physical legacy at the center of Demise. Some have been carefully maintained. Some have changed. Some carry the weight of decisions that will outlast the people who made them.

01

The Mercer Family Home

The old family farmhouse sits back from the road behind a long, tree-lined drive. The white house, fencing, and red barn speak to the family history that existed long before the estate became a source of conflict.

02

Emily's Home — Before

A large two-story home with beige siding, burgundy shutters, a front porch, and the original attached garage before major improvements begin.

03

Emily's Home — Remodeled

The property after extensive exterior upgrades, landscaping, and the addition of a separate three-car garage with living space above.

04

The Pool

A major backyard transformation with a large pool, waterfall, spa, patio, covered entertainment area, and elaborate landscaping.

05

The Duplexes

Two matching duplex buildings sit side by side, professionally maintained with dark siding, red doors, concrete drives, and clean landscaping.

06

The Split-Level Rental

A white split-level rental with burgundy shutters, a red front door, a two-car garage, and mature landscaping.

07

The White Rental

A simple two-story white home with black shutters, a burgundy front door, traditional landscaping, and a white picket fence.

08

The Cedar Rental

A smaller one-story U-shaped residence with cedar siding, mature shade trees, a central drive, and carefully maintained landscaping.

09

Rogersville

The Tennessee town that surrounds the Mercer family story: peaceful, familiar, and close enough that family problems cannot remain hidden forever.
