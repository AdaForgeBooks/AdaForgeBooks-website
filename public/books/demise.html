The Story

Synopsis

For forty years, Samuel Mercer built something modest and lasting on a quiet road in Rogersville, Tennessee: a handful of rental houses, each one restored with his own hands, each tenant treated like family. When he passes quietly in his sleep, he leaves behind a will so carefully planned that his three grown children believe there's nothing left for them to fight over.

They're wrong.

Daniel, the eldest, stays close to home, tending the properties and the people inside them exactly the way his father taught him. Nathan, settled six hours away in Cincinnati, trusts his younger sister completely, checking in from a distance he never imagines will matter. And Emily, the baby of the family, is handed the one role neither of her brothers wanted: executor. Keeper of their father's legacy.

She means to honor it. For a while, she does.

But grief is expensive, and so is trying to look like you're not falling apart. One overdue bill becomes one quiet transfer. One quiet transfer becomes a habit nobody names out loud. And somewhere between a troubled tenant who never should have been trusted and a husband who never stops finding new ways to spend what isn't his, Emily Mercer begins the slow, human process of losing herself inside a lie she never meant to tell.

Two years and six hours of distance won't be enough to let Nathan keep pretending everything is fine. Daniel won't be able to look away from what's happening three houses down his own road forever. And Emily will learn, the hard way, that some debts can't be repaid with an apology, no matter how badly she means it.

Told across alternating points of view and spanning more than three years, Demise is a slow-burn family drama about inheritance, trust, and the particular kind of erosion that happens not in one dramatic moment but in a thousand small, reasonable-sounding decisions. It is a story about what families owe each other, what it costs to look away, and how easily good intentions can be talked into becoming something no one ever meant them to be.
