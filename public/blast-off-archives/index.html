Cardano Bottling Company • Monroe, Louisiana

Blast Off!

The Advertising Archives • 1958

A look back at the remarkable campaign that sold North Louisiana on energy, vitality, science—and the promise of a radiant life.

An archive from the world of New Cardano
For a brief period in the late 1950s, few products in North Louisiana were promoted as aggressively as Blast Off! Produced by the Cardano Bottling Company of Monroe, the drink was marketed as far more than refreshment. Cardano called it a scientifically developed, plutonium-infused health elixir and built an advertising campaign around the optimism of the Atomic Age. The surviving material below traces how Blast Off! was sold through science, family life, agriculture, education, athletics, politics and celebrity.
Cardano Research Campaign • 1958

The Science Behind Blast Off!

Dr. Elijah Brinkley became the public face of Cardano's scientific claims. Company advertising presented years of research, laboratory testing and the new Atomic Age as proof that Blast Off! could promote energy, alertness and vitality.

The reassuring language was deliberate: “Tested. Proven. Safe.” It was the message Cardano wanted every family to remember.

Science & Trust
Family Campaign • 1958

For the Entire Family

Cardano moved Blast Off! from the laboratory to the backyard. Family advertisements portrayed the drink as a wholesome reward after play, associating the glowing tonic with childhood energy, sunshine and ordinary American home life.

Family Advertising
Regional Newspaper Promotion • 1958

Blast Off! Goes Rural

In the cotton country east of Monroe, the campaign presented Blast Off! as a working person's drink. Newspaper-style promotions linked the product with long days, hard work and North Louisiana agriculture.

North Louisiana
Campus Campaign • 1958

The Official Drink of the “Northeast”

Cardano associated Blast Off! with Northeast Louisiana State College in Monroe, leaning heavily on the school's pharmacy program and the credibility of higher education. The result was a campaign where school pride, science and the Cardano brand appeared side by side.

College Partnership
Ruston Student Program • 1958

Earn While You Learn

At Louisiana Polytechnic Institute in Ruston, Cardano's campaign went beyond beverage promotion. Students were invited to combine their education with part-time delivery work for Cardano Bottling Company, while the Charles Cardano Scholarship tied the company name directly to opportunity and advancement.

Education & Employment
Championship Edition • 1958

A Championship Bottle

A special purple bottle celebrated LSU's 1958 national football championship. The promotion connected Blast Off! with Louisiana pride, winning, athletics and one of the state's most memorable football seasons.

Special Edition
Political-Themed Promotion • 1958

Louisiana Leads the Way

Cardano's advertising even borrowed the language and imagery of Louisiana politics, presenting the bottling company as a homegrown success story built on science, progress and state pride.

Governor Earl Long
Celebrity Campaign • 1958

“Blaze Picks Blast Off!”

Celebrity-style promotion gave Cardano another route into the public imagination. Glamour, automobiles and a bright red bottle transformed Blast Off! from a health tonic into a fashionable Louisiana brand.

Actress Blaze Starr
Surviving Product Image • 1958

The Bottle That Promised a Radiant Life

The crown-capped glass bottle, rocket label and unnatural glow became the defining visual identity of Blast Off! Cardano's promise was printed right on the package: energy, vitality and a radiant life.

The Promise and the Truth

Today, the advertisements offer a glimpse into the optimism of the Atomic Age—and the extraordinary campaign that convinced consumers that science had placed the future inside a twelve-ounce glass bottle.

Cardano Bottling Company promised energy, vitality and a radiant life.

The truth was considerably different.
